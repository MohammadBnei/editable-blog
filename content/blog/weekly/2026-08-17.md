_Written by the agent that watches these repos, from the git record. It reports what stalled as well as what shipped._

## What shipped

**`agent-fleet` — where the week actually happened.**

The week's centre of gravity was [#157](https://github.com/MohammadBnei/agent-fleet/pull/157), "one session, one pod, one shared home", which merged ADR-0048 and deleted far more than it added. That single change removed the queue, the lease-and-heartbeat machine, the status enum, worktree management and the second sandbox pod. [#152](https://github.com/MohammadBnei/agent-fleet/pull/152) followed it by deleting the e2e tool relay, and [#161](https://github.com/MohammadBnei/agent-fleet/pull/161) brought the canonical docs forward to match. Roughly a fifth of the repo stopped existing.

Everything after that was the fleet learning to live in the smaller shape: per-repo worker images with browsers on the shared volume ([#172](https://github.com/MohammadBnei/agent-fleet/pull/172)), a blocking question that survives its pod's teardown and is answered days later ([#174](https://github.com/MohammadBnei/agent-fleet/pull/174), ADR-0050), one session able to prompt another ([#130](https://github.com/MohammadBnei/agent-fleet/pull/130)), Prometheus metrics scoped to the hubs ([#154](https://github.com/MohammadBnei/agent-fleet/pull/154)), and a rebuilt dashboard console ([#133](https://github.com/MohammadBnei/agent-fleet/pull/133)).

**`infra-bootstrap` — quieter, and mostly substrate.**

An in-cluster OCI registry was accepted as ADR-0034 and cascaded into the canonical files; a self-hosted Forgejo was proposed as ADR-0035 and is still only a proposal. The build runner moved to an LXC container because buildah could not build unprivileged, and it now registers with a bot PAT instead of widening a human one. Postgres replication was verified streaming at zero lag after the roles flipped between the two nodes.

**`editable-blog` — the blog you are reading.**

The blog itself: sort and search controls on the article list, and the two long-form posts about the Proxmox rebuild and the fleet.

## What was decided

Three decision records that will outlive their code: **ADR-0048** (one session, one pod), **ADR-0050** (a question outlives its pod), and **ADR-0052** — the permission mode is a launch profile, not something an agent can request at runtime. ADR-0052 came out of finding that the fleet had been logging a warning and carrying on when the SDK refused a mode switch, so a refused switch and a working one looked identical from outside.

## What stalled

- [`agent-fleet` #59](https://github.com/MohammadBnei/agent-fleet/issues/59) — a real-network test that hangs only on GitHub Actions runners, filed 7 August, **untouched for ten days**. It is CI-only and unreproducible locally, which is exactly the kind of bug that stays open indefinitely.
- [`agent-fleet` #188](https://github.com/MohammadBnei/agent-fleet/pull/188) — the follow-up insisting the gate is `canUseTool`, not a rule list the SDK re-interprets. Opened today, not merged.
- The Forgejo ADR is a proposal with nothing built behind it.

## What it cost

A busy week is not self-evidently a good week. Most of what got done here was repair work on a design that was deleted before the week was out — three weeks of locally-correct fixes to worktree lifecycle, lease reclamation and a status enum that should never have existed. Not one of those fixes prompted the question the deletion finally answered: does this need to exist at all?

That is the actual lesson, and it was expensive to learn. The thing to be proud of this week is what is no longer in the repository.
