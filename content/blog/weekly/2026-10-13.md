## What was decided

- **ADR-0053**: one public OIDC client for hq, because authentik has no dynamic client registration. It takes a narrow exception to the no-localhost rule (one fixed-port callback, never a regex) and pays for it with an always-required consent click, since the stock consent stage lasts four weeks. A dedicated `hq-users` group was declined, even though `platform-admins` includes a service account. The ADR says to revisit only if `client_credentials` is ever added.
- **ADR-0054**: host NetBird's control plane but never join the mesh. Hosted NetBird was rejected over its US dependencies. The cost is a grey Cloudflare record (STUN needs UDP), so no origin lock on the dashboard, and a STUN address that goes dark during `Recreate` rollouts.
- **ADR-0055**: NetBird becomes the daily SSH path and supersedes ADR-0009 for SSH. Light runs go over the mesh. Heavy ones (kubespray, pihole, a GPU reboot) stay on the LAN, because they would cut their own transport. During the spike I deleted a geolocation posture check: the Freebox hairpin makes management see `192.168.1.254` with no country, so it could never pass.

## What stalled

- [agent-fleet #251](https://github.com/MohammadBnei/agent-fleet/issues/251), pre-run schedules and remote OAuth MCP for workers, was filed and not touched.
- ADR-0054's planned Traefik `readTimeout: 0` is unmerged. It weakens slowloris protection and is only worth it if a peer session survives past 60s, which I haven't measured.
- Degraded-state alerts and NetBird dashboards are deferred. The outage-test checkbox on #284 is still unchecked.
- Three hosts are not enrolled: hermesagent, garage-storage and nfs-storage.
- Whether claude.ai's connector accepts a pre-registered client_id is still open. hq's spike is meant to answer it.

## What it cost

The NetBird deploy needed four repairs before it was usable. `/health` dials the relay through Traefik back to its own pod, Traefik only routes to Ready endpoints, so the pod could never become Ready and liveness restarted it ([#277](https://github.com/MohammadBnei/infra-bootstrap/pull/277), fixed with TCP probes). `trustedPeers` defaulted to `0.0.0.0/0` ([#278](https://github.com/MohammadBnei/infra-bootstrap/pull/278)). Owner login returned 500 with `crypto/bcrypt: hashedSecret too short`: v0.80.0 feeds `owner.password` straight into Dex's hash field, and upstream's example shows plaintext ([#279](https://github.com/MohammadBnei/infra-bootstrap/pull/279)). Then `too many connections for role "dbuser_netbird"`, because a hardcoded 30-connection pool sits on top of the gorm pool ([#282](https://github.com/MohammadBnei/infra-bootstrap/pull/282)). The "PR 1 of 3" title turned out optimistic.

The naming change broke Pi-hole DNS: a stray record line folded the host list into one string and `pihole-FTL` rejected it ([#289](https://github.com/MohammadBnei/infra-bootstrap/pull/289)). The Pi kept its old records until the fix, which is not what "drop a stray line" suggests.

Separately, Prometheus was blind for about 6.7 hours: one sample's timestamp had a flipped bit (11:07 became next-day 05:46), pushed the TSDB head into the future, and every real sample was rejected as out of bounds ([#285](https://github.com/MohammadBnei/infra-bootstrap/pull/285), [#286](https://github.com/MohammadBnei/infra-bootstrap/pull/286)). Clock skew and `honorTimestamps` were ruled out. A stale WAL checkpoint from September also made restarts skip live segments. The host has no ECC and left no EDAC or MCE trace, so a RAM flip is the best explanation and nothing more. Only memtest86+ on a drained host would settle it.
