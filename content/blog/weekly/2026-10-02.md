Window: 2026-09-25 through 2026-10-02.

## What shipped

Wird — Mohammad's own Qur'an companion app, built in about ten days mostly from his phone — got its first public write-up this week. [editable-blog#22](https://github.com/MohammadBnei/editable-blog/pull/22) added the blog post ("reading the Qur'an without speaking Arabic") and a matching portfolio entry linking to the live app and source. The post explains the actual feature: tap a word, see its three-letter root and everything else built on it, then recite a prayer hands-free while an on-device speech model follows along rakʿah by rakʿah. Root glosses are AI-drafted against Lane's Lexicon, never quoted verbatim, then checked against real Qur'anic usage and rated by Arabic speakers. It's the first of three planned posts; the root-matching mechanics and the build process are still to come.

agent-fleet had nothing merge this week — no PRs, no issues, no ADRs.

## What was decided

No ADR landed anywhere this week. The closest thing to a decision is a non-decision, named precisely in infra-bootstrap: the Quran-Lab licence under the zipformer model Wird runs on forbids monetizing anything built on it ([infra-bootstrap#263](https://github.com/MohammadBnei/infra-bootstrap/pull/263)), now written down as a check any future paywall idea has to clear before it ships.

## What stalled

The redistribution terms of that same licence are still unread — gated behind a Hugging Face login the PR didn't chase down. #263 is explicit that this is scoped, not resolved: Wird is clear to keep running as-is, but nobody has verified what redistributing the model would actually require. Separately, the old superseded model weights in `WIRD_MODELS_S3` (roughly 499MB across three stale prefixes) stay in place on purpose, because phones mid-update may still be resuming against an old digest — a cleanup that isn't scheduled, just deferred with a reason attached ([infra-bootstrap#262](https://github.com/MohammadBnei/infra-bootstrap/pull/262)).

## What it cost

The three infra-bootstrap PRs this week ([#262](https://github.com/MohammadBnei/infra-bootstrap/pull/262), [#263](https://github.com/MohammadBnei/infra-bootstrap/pull/263), [#264](https://github.com/MohammadBnei/infra-bootstrap/pull/264)) are the retroactive cost of shipping Wird fast: the bucket serving the zipformer weights was already public and unauthenticated by design before anyone wrote down whose weights they were or under what licence. Writing #262 surfaced #263's licence question, which is still open. #264 applies the same discipline somewhere riskier: the Android release keystore, where the four secrets it documents aren't rotatable like everything else in `docs/secrets.md` — lose that key and every installed user loses the ability to update the app, and loses their local data, permanently. None of this broke this week; it's the paperwork catching up to decisions made while building Wird, after the fact instead of before.
